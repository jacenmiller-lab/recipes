# Scratch Green Bean Casserole

## Instructions

### Crispy shallots

1. Slice the shallots thin and even, using a mandoline or a knife.
2. Heat the beef tallow over medium heat in a deep sauté pan or a medium saucepan. The pan needs to hold enough tallow, deep enough, to fry a decent amount of shallots at once.
3. Fry the shallots in batches, stirring now and then, until golden brown. Expect 6 or more batches so you don't overcrowd the pan.
4. Lift each batch out onto a paper-towel-lined plate and salt it while it's still hot.
5. Add more tallow between batches as needed to keep the level up. After adding fresh tallow, let it come back up to temperature before starting the next batch.

### Bacon mushroom gravy

1. In a large pan, cook the chopped bacon until the fat renders and the bacon is browned. Keep all the fat in the pan, since this big batch needs plenty of gravy.
2. Add the mushrooms and cook until browned.
3. Pour in the chicken broth and heavy cream. Bring to a simmer and let it cook down for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
4. Sprinkle in ½ tsp xanthan gum a little at a time while whisking hard, so it doesn't clump. Simmer 2–3 minutes. If it's still thinner than you want, whisk in up to ½ tsp more. It should coat a spoon, and it will thicken a bit more in the oven.
5. Take it off the heat and season with the Worcestershire, nutritional yeast, salt and pepper.

### Green beans

1. Bring a gallon of water to a boil and add the 1 cup of kosher salt. Set up a big bowl of ice water.
2. Add the green beans and cook for 90 seconds, then move them straight into the ice water. Work in two batches if they don't fit.
3. Once cold, drain them well.

### Assemble and bake

1. Heat the oven to 350°F.
2. In the baking dish, mix the gravy, green beans and half the crispy shallots.
3. Sprinkle parmesan over the top.
4. Bake until hot all the way through and browned on top, about 25–30 minutes.
5. Finish with the rest of the crispy shallots, a few pats of butter, and the chopped chives.

## Notes

- Xanthan gum replaces the almond flour we used to use, which has no starch and barely thickened the gravy. A little goes a long way; too much makes the gravy gummy, so add it gradually.
- The 1 cup of salt is for kosher salt like Diamond Crystal. With Morton kosher or table salt, use about half.
- Oven temperature and bake time are a starting point. The original post doesn't give them.
